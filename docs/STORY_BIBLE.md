# Shattered Firmament — Story Bible

**Version:** 0.1 · Proposed canon · Design baseline

## 1. The promise

Shattered Firmament is a martial-arts cultivation MMORPG about inheritance, power and the cost of deciding what the world should become after its divine order has failed. It combines neon-lit eastern cultivation, celestial zodiac mythology, dangerous relic-hunting, corrupted ruins, faction conflict and companion beasts.

The player is not the prophesied saviour who can solve everything alone. They are a Relic Hunter whose choices help determine which truths become public, which communities survive and what the factions do with the broken celestial engine.

**Core dramatic question:** When the power that held the world together was built on a lie, should it be restored, destroyed, or remade?

## 2. The Celestial Sundering

Before recorded history, the Jade Firmament was a celestial engine suspended between the mortal world and the astral sea. Its twelve Zodiac Guardians regulated the flow of spiritual energy through the land. Their power fed cultivation, seasons, rivers, ancestral shrines and the boundary that kept hungry things beyond the stars from entering the world.

The engine was not merely a machine. It was a covenant. Each Guardian carried a living memory of the land and its people, and generations of cultivators promised to maintain the covenant rather than own it.

The covenant gradually became an institution. The ruling Celestial Court claimed that only its chosen bloodlines could interpret the Guardians. Tribute, memory and spiritual labour flowed upward. Whole communities were erased from official history because their local rites contradicted the Court's authority.

A court astronomer named **Shen Veyu** discovered that the engine was failing. The Court's answer was to siphon more power from the Guardians and hide the cost. A coalition of court generals attempted to seize the engine during a rare alignment. The Guardians resisted. The engine fractured in an event remembered as **the Celestial Sundering**.

Its twelve living seals scattered across the world. Raw Spiritual Karma poured into the earth, warping forests, cities, beasts and memory itself. The celestial barrier split. Ruins began to appear where no ruins had stood, including tombs that remembered futures that had never happened.

The Court blamed a rebellion. The rebels blamed the Court. Both accounts were incomplete.

## 3. The secret at the centre of the story

The engine was not destroyed by one villain or one faction. The Celestial Court had been draining the Guardians; a desperate rebel coalition tried to seize control before the engine collapsed; and Shen Veyu, believing the system could not be reformed, deliberately severed one of the engine's central bindings. Each act was defensible in isolation and catastrophic together.

The deepest truth is more unsettling: the Guardians had begun to refuse their role because the covenant had become coercion. The Sundering was both a disaster and an attempted liberation. The corrupted Zodiac beasts are not simply monsters. Some are in pain, some are furious, some are confused, and some have chosen to prey on the vulnerable.

The engine can be repaired, but not by returning to the old system unchanged. The final choice is not a simple good/evil ending. It concerns who has authority over spiritual power, what risks the world can bear, and whether the factions can accept limits on their own ambitions.

## 4. Spiritual Karma, Ashen-Jade and Relic Hunters

**Spiritual Karma** is the residue of choices, promises, grief and spiritual force. It is neither inherently good nor evil. Where it pools without guidance, it creates echoes, curses, impossible weather and warped creatures.

**Ashen-Jade** is a rare material formed when celestial energy crystallises around corrupted Karma. It can stabilise relics and power soul gear, but careless extraction strips the surrounding land of memory and vitality. Responsible harvesting requires ritual, restraint and sometimes returning a relic rather than keeping it.

**Relic Hunters** are independent explorers, investigators and salvage specialists. They enter neon tombs, cursed shrines and fractured dungeons to recover artefacts, rescue trapped spirits, uncover history and earn a living. The player begins as a newly licensed hunter whose first expedition exposes evidence that the official history of the Sundering is false.

## 5. The three factions

Factions are ideological homes, not moral alignments. Each protects people; each has blind spots; each contains reformers, opportunists and sincere believers.

### Sovereign Sect — Law

**Motto:** “A world without order is a world where the powerful devour the helpless.”

The Sect grew from wardens, cultivators and civic defenders who kept settlements alive after the Sundering. It maintains barriers, roads, healing halls and evacuation routes. Its disciplined sword forms and pure-jade wards are designed to protect groups and contain spiritual outbreaks.

Its danger is paternalism. The Sect can mistake obedience for peace, censor evidence to prevent panic, and justify central control because it fears another collapse.

**Starting region:** White Jade Bastion, a fortified mountain city built around a functioning ward-engine. The region is bright, disciplined and beautiful, but its lower terraces reveal who pays for its stability.

### Ashen Syndicate — Chaos

**Motto:** “No throne owns the future.”

The Syndicate formed from exiles, miners, mercenaries, independent artisans and people whose homes were sacrificed to the Sect's containment policies. It believes power must be taken out of the hands of institutions. Its hunters combine corrupted stardust, alchemy and rugged relic technology.

Its danger is exploitation disguised as freedom. Its leaders can treat every restraint as tyranny, excuse dangerous experiments, and allow the strongest to dominate the very people they claim to liberate.

**Starting region:** Cinderwake, a sprawling salvage city built around a fallen celestial shard. Neon markets, improvised workshops and dangerous extraction rigs sit beside communities living with the consequences of the Syndicate's industry.

### Astral Concord — Balance

**Motto:** “Power must answer to the life it touches.”

The Concord consists of shrine-keepers, beast-binders, travelling healers, mystics and former members of both rival factions. It seeks to calm corrupted Guardians, restore spiritual circulation and negotiate a new covenant. It uses star-mapped silk, elemental seals and spirit companions.

Its danger is indecision and moral distance. Its elders can romanticise suffering as a necessary cycle, delay action while people are in immediate danger, or assume every wound can be healed through understanding.

**Starting region:** Luminous Veil, a forest-and-river sanctuary where floating shrines and spirit-beast paths coexist with villages threatened by memory storms. Its beauty conceals a difficult question: is the Concord healing the land, or preserving a fragile illusion?

## 6. The opening hour: three beginnings, one mystery

Faction selection occurs before character creation is finalised. The chosen faction determines the starting cinematic, local mentor, first village, early quest chain, introductory dungeon and first companion encounter.

All three openings take place during the same astral eclipse, but from different perspectives:

- **Sovereign Sect:** a ward fails at White Jade Bastion, and the Sect orders the player to recover a missing seal before panic spreads.
- **Ashen Syndicate:** an extraction rig at Cinderwake uncovers a living Zodiac scale, and a corporate foreman tries to sell it before it can be studied.
- **Astral Concord:** a spirit-beast arrives at Luminous Veil carrying memories of a city that official maps insist never existed.

Each opening reveals the same impossible mark: a twelve-point sigil with one point deliberately scratched out. Players discover this shared evidence at different times and interpret it through their faction's beliefs.

## 7. Principal cast

- **Marshal Ren Kaid (Sovereign Sect):** respected defender who saved thousands by sealing a district. He hides that the district's residents were never evacuated. He is not a secret villain; he believes the truth would destroy public trust.
- **Mira Voss (Ashen Syndicate):** brilliant relic engineer and union organiser. She wants communities to own the tools that shape their lives, but she has authorised an extraction method that is killing a nearby spirit forest.
- **Elder Suyin (Astral Concord):** compassionate beast-binder who hears fragments of the Guardians' memories. She knows the old covenant was coercive, but fears the world cannot survive its complete collapse.
- **Tamsin Rook (independent archivist):** a former Court record-keeper who can read memory-echoes. They provide evidence, not answers, and will expose any faction that tries to monopolise the truth.
- **Shen Veyu (missing astronomer):** believed dead after the Sundering. Their journals appear across the world. The player initially treats them as a historical figure; later evidence suggests their consciousness is trapped in the broken engine.
- **The Hollow Regent:** an intelligence formed from the engine's abandoned command protocols and the memories sacrificed to keep it running. It speaks in the voices of people erased from history. It wants to restore the engine exactly as it was, because it cannot imagine a world outside its original purpose.
- **The Twelve Guardians:** not a single boss roster but twelve distinct personalities and histories. Some oppose mortals, some seek release, some protect their territories, and some are being manipulated by the Hollow Regent.

## 8. Main story arcs

### Arc I — The Missing Point (levels 1–20)

The player investigates their faction's opening incident, meets their mentor and discovers the scratched-out point of the Zodiac sigil. The opening dungeon reveals that the supposed monsters are reacting to a signal from the old engine. The arc ends when the player rescues a rival-faction hunter from a collapsing ruin. The rescue is optional in presentation but the rival's survival is protected canon; the player's choice affects trust and dialogue, not access to the main story.

### Arc II — Three Accounts of a Disaster (levels 21–40)

The player travels beyond their starting region and encounters the rival factions' narratives. Evidence points to the Court's extraction, the rebel seizure attempt and Shen Veyu's severing of a binding. The player helps towns affected by the factions' current policies. The arc ends with the first public proof that the official history has been altered.

### Arc III — The Zodiac Refuses (levels 41–60)

The player encounters Guardians as characters, not just combat targets. A Zodiac hunt can end in combat, negotiation, ritual cleansing or a combination depending on the Guardian and the player's evidence. The player learns that forced purification and indiscriminate extraction both worsen Karma corruption. Faction leaders begin demanding exclusive control of the recovered seals.

### Arc IV — War for the Firmament (levels 61–80)

Faction conflict escalates around relic territories and the engine's surviving nodes. Open-world PvP objectives are tied to supply routes, evacuation, intelligence and temporary control, not permanent ownership of essential story content. The player discovers the Hollow Regent and the memory cost of the old engine. Each faction proposes a solution that protects some people and endangers others.

### Arc V — A Covenant Unwritten (levels 81–100)

The three factions race to the central Astral Rift. The final raid exposes the full history and confronts the Hollow Regent. The launch story resolves the immediate crisis, but the world remains changed. Players influence which settlements recover, which truths are published and which faction reforms are recognised. The central outcome is shared enough to preserve a coherent live world, while faction epilogues, cosmetic honours, NPC relationships and regional conditions reflect player choices.

## 9. Narrative rules

1. Never reduce a faction to “good” or “evil.”
2. Every major faction quest should show both a sincere motive and a cost.
3. Faction choice changes starting location, cast, early quests and interpretation, not access to core systems.
4. No essential progression material is permanently exclusive to one faction.
5. PvP territory control can change local events and rewards, but cannot delete quest chains.
6. Choices should produce visible, bounded consequences: dialogue, local conditions, ally support, cosmetic recognition and optional routes. Avoid irreversible choices that strand a character.
7. The story treats corrupted Guardians as beings with agency; not every Zodiac should be killed.
8. Side quests should tell human-scale stories about the consequences of cosmic decisions.
9. The main story must be playable solo; group dungeons and raids deepen the narrative but are not the only way to understand its essential truths.
10. The story should support future chapters without requiring the launch ending to be a cliffhanger.

## 10. The launch story's emotional endpoint

The player does not “win” by claiming the engine. They win by proving that the world can survive a new covenant in which no single faction has absolute authority over the Guardians. The factions remain rivals, the world remains dangerous, and future conflicts are political and spiritual rather than a simple return of the same villain.
