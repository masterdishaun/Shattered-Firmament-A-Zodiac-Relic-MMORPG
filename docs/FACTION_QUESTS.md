# Shattered Firmament — Faction Starts & Side Quest Catalogue

**Version:** 0.1 · Proposed content plan

## Design goals

- Each faction begins in a visually and emotionally distinct home region.
- Each faction gets its own mentor, local conflict, early dungeon, companion encounter and side-quest chain.
- Quests expose the faction's ideals and blind spots through ordinary people, not exposition alone.
- Faction-specific content is substantial but not permanently exclusive in power rewards.
- Most side quests are solo-friendly, with optional group variants for elite encounters.
- Quest outcomes can alter local dialogue, services, NPC trust and cosmetic rewards, but must not lock core progression.
- Rewards emphasise EXP, Silver, crafting materials, lore, titles, cosmetics, companion affinity and account-wide recipes.

# A. Sovereign Sect — White Jade Bastion

## Starting arc: “The Ward That Lied” (levels 1–20)

**Opening:** During an eclipse, a ward-engine beneath White Jade Bastion fails. The player is ordered by Marshal Ren Kaid to recover a stolen jade seal. The official explanation blames Syndicate saboteurs. Investigation reveals that the ward's power has been diverted to keep the upper city bright while the lower terraces suffer spiritual sickness.

**Main beats**

1. The First Bell — learn movement, combat, interaction and ward-based abilities.
2. A Perfect Wall — escort ward engineers and defend a barrier from Karma echoes.
3. Names Beneath the Stone — find records of residents officially listed as evacuated.
4. The Missing Seal — enter the first solo-friendly dungeon, the Underterrace Archive.
5. The Enemy in the Ruins — rescue a wounded Syndicate hunter and discover the scratched Zodiac sigil.
6. The Marshal's Choice — confront Ren with the evidence. The player can publish it, deliver it privately or ask Tamsin Rook to verify it. Ren remains an ally, but trust and dialogue vary.

**Arc payoff:** the Sect begins a public inquiry, but some officers resist. The player earns the title **Bastion Witness** and a cosmetic jade ward effect.

## Sovereign side quests

### S1. The Names We Sealed (levels 5–10)

**Hook:** A clerk asks the player to recover names from a sealed evacuation ledger before it is destroyed.
**Objectives:** enter a protected archive; recover three memory tablets; identify families still living in the lower terraces; deliver copies to relatives.
**Choice:** give the original to the Sect's inquiry or publish a verified copy through the neutral archivist.
**Reveals:** the ward failure was hidden to prevent panic, but the cover-up also erased victims from relief records.
**Rewards:** EXP, Silver, Bastion reputation, account recipe for Ward-Lacquer.
**Consequence:** memorial NPCs appear in the lower city; the main story remains available regardless of choice.

### S2. The Price of a Quiet Street (levels 8–14)

**Hook:** A district is peaceful because wardens confiscate residents' relics without explaining why.
**Objectives:** interview three households; inspect confiscated items; discover one relic is genuinely unstable while two were harmless heirlooms.
**Choice:** help the wardens build a transparent inspection process or return the harmless relics first.
**Reveals:** security policy can protect lives and still become abusive.
**Rewards:** crafting components, cosmetic shoulder insignia and a local service discount.

### S3. A Sword for the Unarmed (levels 12–18)

**Hook:** A retired sword instructor trains evacuees but is ordered to stop by a commander worried about unrest.
**Objectives:** train NPC volunteers; defend a supply convoy; demonstrate that the volunteers can protect themselves without joining the Sect.
**Choice:** endorse a community watch or a Sect-led training charter.
**Reveals:** protection is strongest when communities have agency.
**Rewards:** combat technique tutorial, title **Lantern Guard**, companion affinity.

### S4. The Marshal's Unsent Letters (levels 16–20)

**Hook:** Ren's courier disappears carrying letters to families from the sealed district.
**Objectives:** track the courier through a memory storm; recover the letters; choose whether to read them before delivery.
**Reveals:** Ren signed the sealing order believing a second evacuation would trigger a breach. He still bears responsibility, but was not indifferent.
**Rewards:** story lore, unique non-stat cosmetic sword trail and a dialogue option in Arc II.

## Sovereign early dungeon: Underterrace Archive

A solo story instance with optional difficulty. Rooms are arranged around official records, deleted names and ward mechanisms. Boss: **The Census Warden**, an archive construct that attacks anyone not recognised by its incomplete registry. The resolution is to restore the missing names and disable the hostile protocol, not merely destroy the construct.

# B. Ashen Syndicate — Cinderwake

## Starting arc: “The Shard That Sang” (levels 1–20)

**Opening:** An extraction rig uncovers a living Zodiac scale. The player is hired to secure the scale before a foreman sells it to an unknown buyer. The rig's workers begin hearing memories that do not belong to them. Mira Voss wants the site shut down, while Syndicate leaders argue that the city cannot survive without the income.

**Main beats**

1. Sparks Before Dawn — learn combat, salvage and improvised relic tools.
2. The Rig Is Still Hungry — rescue workers as the rig overloads.
3. A Scale with a Pulse — examine the living scale and learn it responds to fear.
4. The Cinder Vault — enter a solo-friendly extraction dungeon.
5. A Contract Written in Smoke — expose the foreman's illegal sale and discover the scratched Zodiac sigil.
6. Freedom Has a Price — help Mira negotiate a workers' vote on the site's future.

**Arc payoff:** Cinderwake suspends the most dangerous extraction method but debates how to replace the lost income. The player earns **Shardbreaker** and a cosmetic ember-tech weapon effect.

## Ashen Syndicate side quests

### A1. The Last Shift (levels 5–10)

**Hook:** Workers are trapped behind a failing extraction gate; the company wants the player to save the expensive equipment first.
**Objectives:** stabilise the gate; rescue workers; recover medical supplies; document unsafe orders.
**Choice:** publish the evidence immediately or secure a compensation fund before release.
**Reveals:** the foreman exploited the workforce, but the whole district relies on the rig's income.
**Rewards:** EXP, Silver, salvage materials, account recipe for Ember Rivets.

### A2. A Market for Stolen Memories (levels 8–14)

**Hook:** A black-market dealer sells memory fragments taken from corrupted ruins.
**Objectives:** trace a memory buyer; identify fragments belonging to living families; recover the most dangerous fragment.
**Choice:** dismantle the market or help create a consent-based archive for harmless memories.
**Reveals:** some buyers seek comfort and lost history; others use memories to blackmail survivors.
**Rewards:** lore collection, marketplace reputation, cosmetic mask.

### A3. The Price of a Free Hand (levels 12–18)

**Hook:** A neighbourhood collective refuses to pay an extraction guild's protection fee and is threatened by hired muscle.
**Objectives:** investigate the fee; defend a workshop; negotiate a public charter.
**Choice:** back a worker-run cooperative or an independent mutual-aid network.
**Reveals:** freedom without safeguards can leave weaker people at the mercy of private power.
**Rewards:** crafting materials, account-wide workshop recipe and title **Cinder Advocate**.

### A4. The Beast in the Furnace (levels 16–20)

**Hook:** A small celestial beast has nested in a furnace and is keeping it from exploding, but its presence is ruining production.
**Objectives:** inspect the furnace; gather non-harmful stabilisers; calm the beast; convince the foreman to redesign the line.
**Reveals:** not every obstacle is an enemy; the beast is drawn to the scale's pain.
**Rewards:** companion affinity, a cosmetic pet accessory and dialogue options in Arc II.

## Ashen early dungeon: The Cinder Vault

A solo story instance through a failing industrial complex where machinery has fused with celestial bone. Boss: **The Furnace Saint**, a worker whose armour was fused to the rig during an earlier disaster. The encounter can be resolved by interrupting the extraction loop and freeing the trapped spirit; defeating it violently is possible in combat but yields a less complete story resolution, never exclusive essential power.

# C. Astral Concord — Luminous Veil

## Starting arc: “The Forest That Remembered” (levels 1–20)

**Opening:** A spirit-beast arrives at a river shrine carrying memories of a city erased from maps. Elder Suyin asks the player to follow its trail and calm a spreading memory storm. The storm reveals villagers reliving the same evening, including the moment their ancestors were ordered to surrender their names to the old engine.

**Main beats**

1. A Bell Beneath the Water — learn elemental seals, movement and spirit interaction.
2. The Borrowed Evening — help villagers separate memory echoes from present danger.
3. Footprints in Starlight — follow the beast through a shifting forest.
4. The Drowned Shrine — enter a solo-friendly memory dungeon.
5. The Guardian's Refusal — hear a Zodiac echo reject forced binding.
6. Mercy Without Consent — confront the Concord elder about a ritual performed without the villagers' consent.

**Arc payoff:** the Concord changes its rite to require community consent, though elders disagree about the risk. The player earns **Veil Listener** and a cosmetic constellation aura.

## Astral Concord side quests

### C1. The Names the River Keeps (levels 5–10)

**Hook:** A river returns objects bearing the names of people who officially never existed.
**Objectives:** recover three objects; find their living descendants; perform a remembrance rite.
**Choice:** preserve the memories in a public shrine or return each memory to the family.
**Reveals:** remembrance can heal grief, but unwanted memories can reopen trauma.
**Rewards:** EXP, Silver, spirit herbs, account recipe for Memory Thread.

### C2. A Beast Is Not a Tool (levels 8–14)

**Hook:** A young beast-binder has captured a corrupted fox spirit to protect a village.
**Objectives:** investigate the attacks; discover the fox is guarding its injured litter; defend villagers while opening an escape route.
**Choice:** help relocate the fox family or build a protected sanctuary at the forest edge.
**Reveals:** protection can become imprisonment when the protected being has no choice.
**Rewards:** companion affinity, pet cosmetic and title **Gentle Hand**.

### C3. The Healer Who Would Not Choose (levels 12–18)

**Hook:** A healer tries to treat both a village and a corrupted shrine, but delays are putting both at risk.
**Objectives:** gather supplies; triage urgent cases; recruit volunteers; help the healer choose an order of treatment.
**Choice:** prioritise the immediate human crisis or stabilise the shrine first, with the other task completed by allies.
**Reveals:** compassion needs decisions and accountability, not only good intentions.
**Rewards:** healing consumable recipe, crafting materials and local trust.

### C4. The Quiet That Costs Too Much (levels 16–20)

**Hook:** A Concord ritual calms a region but also suppresses residents' memories and anger.
**Objectives:** interview affected villagers; test the ritual's boundary; find a way to reduce the storm without erasing testimony.
**Choice:** suspend the rite pending consent or keep a limited version with public oversight.
**Reveals:** peace imposed through silence is not the same as healing.
**Rewards:** lore, cosmetic spirit trail and dialogue options in Arc II.

## Astral early dungeon: The Drowned Shrine

A solo story instance in which rooms shift between the shrine's past and present. Boss: **The Echo-Bound Guardian**, a spirit forced to repeat its final command. The encounter resolves when the player reconstructs the interrupted covenant and offers the Guardian a choice; combat remains available if it becomes hostile.

# D. Shared side quests and cross-faction threads

These quests become available after the opening arcs. Their local presentation and dialogue reflect the player's faction, but all three factions can complete them.

### X1. The Cartographer of Missing Roads (levels 21–30)

An independent cartographer maps roads that appear only in memory storms. The player escorts surveyors, recovers route markers and discovers that several maps were deliberately edited after the Sundering. Rewards: exploration EXP, map cosmetics and access to optional ruins.

### X2. The Price of Ashen-Jade (levels 25–35)

A settlement's wells are losing their memories after nearby extraction. Players investigate the mine, test alternative stabilisation methods and negotiate compensation. The solution supports a safer extraction protocol; no faction can monopolise the recipe.

### X3. Twelve Plates, Eleven Names (levels 30–40)

A ruined observatory contains twelve Zodiac plates, but one has been deliberately scratched out. The player compares three faction archives and learns that the missing point was the engine's consent seal. Rewards: lore, crafting materials and a main-story clue.

### X4. The Hunter Who Came Back (levels 35–45)

A rival-faction hunter returns from a dungeon carrying a false memory of the player's faction. Players investigate, clear the hunter's name and uncover a protocol used to manipulate faction hostility. Rewards: trust, a title and a route into an optional elite instance.

### X5. A Feast Without Flags (levels 40–50)

Two displaced communities refuse to share supplies because each blames the other faction. The player coordinates cooks, gathers food and uncovers a quartermaster who profited from the conflict. Rewards: community reputation, food recipes and a neutral social event.

### X6. The Guardian's First Name (levels 45–55)

A wounded Zodiac echo asks the player to recover its original name from three different memory sources. Players learn that each faction preserved only the part of history that supported its worldview. Rewards: a Zodiac lore entry and a non-stat visual effect.

### X7. The Auction of Tomorrow (levels 55–65)

A broker sells predictions from a future that may never happen. The player investigates market manipulation, protects buyers and exposes forged prophecies. Rewards: tradeable materials and marketplace tutorial; the quest never grants market-control privileges.

### X8. The Village That Voted No (levels 60–70)

A village rejects all three factions' plans for a nearby relic site. The player helps residents design a local stewardship agreement, then defends the village from a threat without overriding its decision. Rewards: a regional service unlock and a cosmetic banner.

### X9. Twelve Shadows at Dusk (levels 70–80)

A group dungeon shows twelve possible outcomes of the Sundering. Players collect evidence for Tamsin Rook and learn that the Hollow Regent is restoring old engine protocols. Rewards: raid preparation materials and story access, with an alternate solo investigation route for the essential narrative.

### X10. The Last Unwritten Page (levels 85–95)

Players assemble fragments of Shen Veyu's final journal. Each faction tries to frame the evidence to validate its own solution. The player chooses how to publish the findings, affecting faction epilogues and public trust but not access to the final raid.

# E. Quest system requirements

Every quest record should eventually contain:

- Stable quest ID and content version.
- Faction requirement or shared availability.
- Level band and prerequisite IDs.
- Solo/group/instance classification.
- Dialogue, objectives and completion conditions.
- Choice flags and bounded consequences.
- Reward table and tradeability rules.
- Repeatability, reset policy and catch-up eligibility.
- Accessibility and localisation notes.
- Analytics events for start, objective progress, abandon, completion and choice.

Quest progression and reward grants must be server-authoritative and idempotent. Content must not rely on daily streaks. Faction-specific rewards should be primarily narrative, cosmetic, reputation or alternate-route rewards; no faction-exclusive competitive power.
